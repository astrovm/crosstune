Crosstune Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 7, 2026

In short

  • No account, ads or analytics.
  • There's no Crosstune server. Your history stays on your phone.
  • Links you convert go only to the music services involved.
  • Recognizing a song sends Shazam only a fingerprint of the sound, never the audio.
  • Clear history anytime in the app.

Details

Crosstune is an Android app developed by astrovm that opens music links in the music app or website you choose. This policy explains what information the app uses and where it goes.

No account, advertising, or analytics

Crosstune does not require an account and does not include advertising, analytics, or a crash-reporting service. The developer does not operate a server that receives your music links or listening history. Crosstune does not sell your information.

Information on your device

The app stores your settings and recent resolved links on your device. Recent entries can include the source link, title, artist, artwork URL, and prepared destination links. These preferences and history are excluded from the app's Android cloud-backup and device-transfer rules.

Crosstune reads a link you paste, share to it, or open through Android link handling. Its clipboard shortcuts read clipboard text when you invoke them. It checks which supported music apps are installed so it can offer them as destinations. That information stays on your device.

Requests to music services

To resolve a music link, Crosstune requests its public page or public API from the source service. It also loads cover artwork from the service's image servers. Supported source services include Spotify, YouTube, YouTube Music, Apple Music, Deezer, TIDAL, SoundCloud, Bandcamp, and Audiomack. Shazam links are read through Apple Music's lookup, and older ones through Shazam's public API first. Shared Google results are followed on Google to read the song's title.

When “Open the exact match” is enabled, the app sends the item's title and artist to the destination's search service: Apple Music, Deezer, YouTube Music, YouTube, or Bandcamp. You can disable this option in Settings. When you open a destination, the selected app or website receives the destination link or search terms. When you use Play all on a playlist or album, the app sends each song’s title and artist to YouTube Music’s search, then sends the list of videos found to YouTube, which returns a temporary playlist. Custom destinations receive the search terms in the URL template you configure. Sharing a result sends it to the app or recipient you select.

When you type a song's name instead of a link, Crosstune sends the name to Deezer's and Apple's public song searches, and shows the songs they find, so you can open the song you meant without its link. When you ask for a song's lyrics, the app sends the song's title and artist to LRCLIB, a lyrics service that needs no account or key.

If you let lyrics follow the song playing in your music app, Android asks you to allow Crosstune's notification access. Crosstune uses it only to see which song your music apps say they're playing and how far into it they are, so the lyrics can follow along and a line you tap can move the song there. It never reads your notifications, and none of this leaves your phone. You can turn it off in Android's settings at any time.

Like other internet requests, these requests expose your IP address and normal request information to the contacted service. Those services may retain request logs and process information under their own privacy policies. The developer doesn't control how they process or keep that data. A music service may require its own account or subscription after you open it.

Song recognition

When you ask Crosstune to recognize a song, it records sound from the microphone only while it listens, for up to 12 seconds, and stops as soon as it finds the song. On your phone, it turns the sound into a fingerprint of its loudest points. Only that fingerprint goes to Shazam, never the audio, and the recording isn't saved. Crosstune doesn't send your location. Shazam answers with the song's title, artist, cover and Apple Music ID, and Crosstune then looks the song up like a pasted link. Like other requests, this one exposes your IP address to Shazam, which processes it under its own privacy policy. In Settings, you can choose Shazam or the Google app to recognize songs instead; they listen under their own privacy policies.

Permissions and security

Crosstune uses Android's internet permission to retrieve music information and artwork. It asks for the microphone permission the first time you recognize a song, and uses the microphone only while listening. It does not request location, camera, contacts, or advertising-ID permissions. Built-in music-service requests use HTTPS. If you configure a custom destination, choose a trusted HTTPS address.

Retention and deletion

You can clear recent history in the app. Clearing Crosstune's app storage in Android Settings removes its local settings and history. The developer holds no server-side account or listening-history record to delete. Requests already received by third-party music services are subject to those services' retention and deletion policies.

Children

Crosstune does not ask users for their age or personal profile information and does not knowingly collect children's personal information on a developer-operated server. Linked music services have their own age requirements and privacy policies. Parents and guardians should review those before a child uses them.

Contact and changes

For privacy questions, contact ~@4st.li. If you email the developer, the email service processes your address and message so the developer can respond. Please don't include sensitive information you don't need to share.
