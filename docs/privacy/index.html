Crosstune Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 20, 2026

In short

  • No account, ads or analytics.
  • Your history stays on your phone. Crosstune's only server gets the problem reports you send, and automatic ones if you turn them on. It keeps no IP address and deletes each report after 180 days.
  • Links you convert go only to the music services involved.
  • Recognizing a song sends Shazam only a fingerprint of the sound, never the audio.
  • Clear history anytime in the app.
  • AI features are optional. They send lyrics to OpenAI only after you sign in with ChatGPT.

Details

Crosstune is an Android app by astrovm that opens music links in the app you choose and shows the lyrics of what you play. This page says what the app uses and where it goes.

No account, ads or analytics

Crosstune has no account, advertising, analytics or crash reporting, and doesn't sell your information. The developer's only server gets the problem reports you choose to send, described below. Every other request goes from your phone straight to the service named here. Like any request, it shows that service your IP address, and the service handles it under its own privacy policy.

On your phone

Your settings, recent songs, saved lines and practice cards stay on your phone, and are left out of Android's backups. Songs you keep for offline keep their lyrics, translations, chords and covers on the phone, fetched on Wi-Fi. Crosstune reads a link only when you open, share or paste it, and the clipboard only when you use a paste shortcut. It checks which music apps are installed to offer them.

Opening links

To name a link's song, Crosstune reads its public page or API on the service it's from: Spotify, YouTube, YouTube Music, Apple Music, Deezer, TIDAL, SoundCloud, Bandcamp, Audiomack, Shazam or Google, and loads its cover. To open it in another app, the title and artist go to that app's search. When that search isn't sure, they also go to Apple's and Deezer's song searches. For Spotify, they go to ListenBrainz, and the artist's name to MusicBrainz, and Spotify's public pages are read. Play all and Shuffle do this for each song, and YouTube makes the playlist from the videos found. A song name you type goes to Deezer's and Apple's searches. A custom destination gets the search words in the address you set.

Lyrics, chords and translations

A song's title and artist go to LRCLIB, YouTube Music, NetEase Cloud Music, Letras and Genius for its lyrics, to Ultimate Guitar, Cifra Club, LaCuerda, U-FRET, J-Total and ChordWiki for its chords, to Apple to find the original of a cover, and to UtaTen for the readings of a Japanese song. Lines you translate go to Google Translate, or to MyMemory, and a word you look up goes to them or to Wiktionary. Lyrics already in your language aren't sent. Readings are worked out on your phone. ChordWiki opens in a hidden browser page, which runs its scripts, can keep cookies in the app's browser storage, and may contact Cloudflare and Google. A Japanese dictionary from Maven Central and a speech model from Hugging Face download once, on Wi-Fi.

Following your music

With notification access, Crosstune sees which song your music apps play and how far in, so the lyrics follow along and its player can pause, skip or like the song in your app. For Simple Radio, it also checks that the radio's media notification is showing, without reading its text. This stays on your phone. A practice card plays its line in an app that already has the song open, or in a small YouTube player on the card, which finds the song's video as a link does. You aren't signed in to YouTube there. For Radio Garden, Crosstune sends Apple Music the parts of the station's current-song text that look like a title and artist, to check if they name a song.

Listening

Crosstune listens only while you name a song, while lyrics are open or floating, and, once you allowed the microphone, while it's open and a playing app doesn't say which song it is. It hears the sound your phone plays, more cleanly with Shizuku or root, and uses the microphone only when the phone won't share its sound. Android shows when the microphone is on. Only a fingerprint of the sound goes to Shazam, never the audio, and Shazam answers with the song. Apple is then asked how long that recording is. Offline, the fingerprint waits in the app's memory until you're back online. If Shazam can't be reached, the Shazam or Google app opens instead, when you have one.

Lining lyrics up with the singing, timing lyrics that have no timing, and checking whether a song is sung, all happen on your phone, and nothing is sent. MilkDrop visuals read the sound too, to move with it. The sound is never saved.

ChatGPT

The AI features are optional, and need your own paid ChatGPT plan. You sign in on OpenAI's site. The sign-in stays on your phone, out of backups, and goes only to OpenAI. While you're signed in, OpenAI gets what each feature needs: the song, its lyrics, the words and lines you ask about, and a description you type. When a search for a song isn't sure, the song's title and artist and what the search found go to OpenAI too, so it picks the right one. A song's meaning also asks MusicBrainz for its year and album.

Problem reports

Report a problem, in the lyrics, sends the developer's server, when you tap Send, what you write, the Crosstune and Android versions, the song's title, artist and length, where its lyrics came from and how they're timed, the words and times of each line you put in time by hand (at most the 300 that tell the most, fewer to stay under 48 KB), and the app Crosstune follows. A report about timing also sends the package name of the app playing the song, its playback speed, how far ahead Crosstune shows the words, and the last minute of Crosstune's own timing notes, with Shazam's number for the recording. Nothing about you, your other apps or your phone is in them. The server runs on Cloudflare, which handles the connection, your IP address included, under its own privacy policy. The server doesn't keep your IP address. To limit how many reports one network can send, it counts them for the day under a code made with a secret key from the network part of the address, and deletes those counts the next day; the database's backups keep them up to 30 days more. Without the key, a code can't be traced back to the network. Only the developer reads reports, and they're never made public. Each is deleted after 180 days, and the database's backups keep it up to 30 days more. With Report song problems automatically on in Settings, or once you say yes when Crosstune asks, it sends the same details, without a comment, when it finds a song with no lyrics, timing or chords, when you put a song's words in time by hand, or when the singing it hears is off from the words, once per song. What you put in time by hand waits on your phone until then, whatever you answered. Timings an earlier version of Crosstune kept go once each too: the song's name, recording and length, the times of its lines, and whether the singing was heard. Problems Crosstune finds and words you put in time by hand go together, at most 23 in a day; the rest wait on your phone for a later day. A report Crosstune sends by itself that can't get there waits on your phone for the next start: the latest 20 put in time by hand, the latest 20 problems found, and every timing an earlier version kept. One the server never takes is deleted and never sent again. Each one kept from this version on carries a random id, made for that report alone and saying nothing about you or your phone, so the server keeps it once if it gets there twice. Turning automatic reports off deletes the problems found that wait; what you put in time by hand and the timings an earlier version kept stay, and go only once reports are on again. A report you send yourself with Send isn't kept: if it can't get there, Crosstune says so. Send it again unchanged and the same id goes, so it's kept once. In Settings, Report a problem opens a GitHub issue or an email instead, for you to send, under GitHub's or your email service's privacy policy.

Marking a word's reading wrong, in the word's details, sends the same server, when you tap Send, the word, the reading shown, what made it (like a dictionary on your phone, the lyrics themselves or ChatGPT), the whole line of lyrics it's in, its script, the right reading if you type one, the song's title and artist, where its lyrics came from, the Crosstune and Android versions, and a random id, made for that report alone. It's kept and read like the reports above.

Permissions

Crosstune uses the internet, and the network state to tell when you're offline. It asks for the microphone the first time you name a song or open lyrics. Notification access lets lyrics follow your music. Floating lyrics ask to show over other apps and to show a notification. Practice reminders ask to show notifications, and set their daily alarm again after a restart. Crosstune doesn't ask for your location, camera, contacts or advertising ID. With Shizuku or root, it only changes these settings for Crosstune, and lets lyrics hear what music apps play. Built-in requests use HTTPS, so choose an HTTPS address for a custom destination.

Browser extension

The extension for Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox works only on the music sites it supports. It sends a link's title and artist where the app does, and, only while its lyrics panel is open, to LRCLIB, YouTube Music, NetEase Cloud Music, Letras and Genius. Its settings stay in your browser, and it sends nothing to the developer.

Deleting your data

Clear your history in the app, or clear Crosstune's storage in Android's settings to remove everything it keeps on your phone. The developer holds no account or history about you. Reports carry nothing that tells who sent them, so one can't be found to delete on request. The services Crosstune contacts keep their requests under their own policies.

Children

Crosstune doesn't ask your age and doesn't knowingly collect children's information. The music services it opens have their own age rules and privacy policies.

Contact and changes

For privacy questions, write to crosstune@4st.li. Your email service handles your address and message so the developer can answer. This policy changes when the app does, with a new date on this page.
